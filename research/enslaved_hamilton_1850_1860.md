# Enslaved people in Hamilton, 1850-1860: a working list (19 Nov 2026)

`enslaved_hamilton_1850_1860.csv` lists every enslaved person the sources
read so far place in a Hamilton household, one row per person. **Research
only: nothing in `data/` and nothing on the site.**

## What it holds

| group | rows | source |
|---|---|---|
| 1860, living in a Hamilton household | 105 | 1860 slave schedule, the enumerator's Hamilton route (film 005171762, images 127 R3 to 129 L6), every line read on the full-resolution image |
| 1860, Hamilton connection only | 3 | the lines just before the route: a woman owned by M. Dowel of Hamilton, hired out; two hired from M. Chamblin by Eli Tavenner |
| 1850, same-name holders | 37 | 1850 slave schedule (film 004206479, images 15-16), holders with the same name as an 1860 Hamilton holder |
| named | 7 | the will of James Decatur Heaton (1858-59) and the 1859 death register |

**The 1860 rows are the town list.** The route is fixed by the free schedule:
its Hamilton pages (film 005171728, images 377-389) run from Jonah Hatcher to
Peter Steptoe, and the slave schedule follows the same households in the same
order. Of the 105, **67 were held by 18 Hamilton households** and **38 were
hired into Hamilton households from 32 owners**, most of them in Leesburg,
Waterford, Round Hill, Aldie and Hillsboro. Page 38's own total (65) agrees
with the rows read from it.

- 56 female, 49 male; 32 under ten; 24 marked mulatto, the rest black.
- **The schedule names no one.** Ages, sex and colour are all it gives.

**The 1850 rows cannot be added to the 1860 ones.** The 1850 schedule records
no town and no hiring, so a "Hamilton" list for 1850 cannot be drawn. These
are the holdings of men and women with the same names as 1860 Hamilton
holders. Many are probably the same people ten years younger, which a
schedule without names cannot prove.

## How the 1860 pages are written

A line reading "*X* Emp" with no numbers names an employer. The "Owner *Y*"
line or lines after it carry the people hired. Where "Emp" is written small
on a holder's last line, the holder also hired the people on the next owner
lines; those rows say "probable". Small place names under an owner's name are
the owner's residence.

## Names

- **Sam, Catharine, and her children Adelaide and Martha.** These are named in
  the will of James Decatur Heaton, dated 6 Dec 1858 and proved 15 Mar 1859.
  He left them with his wife Cecelia, the 1860 holder of 11. The will also
  allows the sale of his other, unnamed "negroes" to pay debts. Sources: Will
  Book 2-N, pp.166-169, and FamilySearch full text, image group 007645060.
- **Maria**, about 50, enslaved by Malinda Dowell. She died of consumption in
  July 1859 at her mistress's residence (Register of Deaths 1853-1865, image
  61, line 80).
- **Flora**, enslaved by James D. Heaton. She died of scarlet fever in April
  1859, most likely aged 9 months, and her mother was **Agnes** (same page,
  line 82).
